Extended Cognitive Architecture

Intelligence not as a fixed state, but as a system that turns intention into action, outcomes into information, and error into correction.

PortuguêsEnglishEspañol

Core thesis

A human brain can serve as the intentional nucleus of a broader cognitive system. AI models, software agents, databases, sensors, and verification mechanisms can extend memory, search, simulation, and the ability to act.

The value of the system does not depend on claiming perfect intelligence or control over the future. It depends on a verifiable circuit that can detect error, incorporate evidence, and alter the next decision.

Loop: Intention → Perception → Hypotheses → Decision → Action → Outcome → Feedback → Correction → New intention

Accumulated intelligence

Accumulated intelligence is the ability to reduce, over time, the distance between making an error, detecting it, and correcting behavior. An insight becomes more valuable when it stops existing only in a conversation and becomes a reusable procedure, code, memory, metric, or agent.

Operational principle: do not optimize only for the quality of one answer. Optimize the speed and reliability with which real outcomes become better decisions.

Architecture

  1. Human nucleus: defines goals, values, priorities, and interprets real-world consequences.
  2. Perception: collects relevant data from digital and physical environments.
  3. Modeling: generates competing hypotheses and estimates uncertainty.
  4. Critique: searches for contradictions, bias, and missing evidence.
  5. Decision: selects actions proportional to confidence and risk.
  6. Execution: turns decisions into controlled, auditable tasks.
  7. Feedback: measures what actually happened.
  8. Memory: preserves context, outcomes, errors, and useful rules.

Reality rule

Sensations, intuitions, models, and predictions can be useful inputs, but they are not automatic proof about the external world. The more extraordinary a hypothesis is, the stronger the independent test should be before acting as though it were true.

Operational authority should grow with verifiable performance, not status or self-description. The system should record predictions, confidence, decisions, and outcomes so it can learn from the gap between expectation and reality.

Leverage

Data, money, computation, and models are resources. They become leverage when they participate in a learning loop. The key transition occurs when a good line of reasoning is crystallized into infrastructure: something that keeps observing, comparing, measuring, and improving after the original conversation ends.

Authorship and scope

This page records a conceptual formulation attributed to Guilherme Peralta Novaes. It does not claim that each component is individually unprecedented, nor does it claim proof of artificial consciousness, superintelligence, infallibility, or the ability to control the future.

The proposal is operational: combine human cognition and computational systems in measurable, correctable, progressively reusable learning loops.
